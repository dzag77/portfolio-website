HQ Enterprise Product Redesign

Information architecture Workflow redesign

About Project

HQ is a complex enterprise ground transportation platform serving employees, travel bookers, administrators, and corporate mobility teams.

The redesign explores how the product could be reorganized to simplify complex workflows, reduce duplication, improve discoverability, and create a scalable foundation for future growth.

The Challenge

As HQ expanded, related features became scattered across the product, increasing navigation complexity and making core workflows harder to understand.

The challenge was to simplify the experience without redesigning the entire platform, focusing on the information architecture and the primary booking workflow.

Rethinking the Information Architecture

The existing navigation had grown around individual features rather than clear user tasks.

Related functionality was spread across multiple areas, while supporting and administrative tools competed with core workflows at the same navigation level.

Principles Behind the New Structure

  • Group functionality by user task, not by individual feature
  • Keep the primary navigation focused on the product’s main workflows
  • Place supporting functionality close to the workflow where it is used
  • Consolidate overlapping employee and administration controls
  • Use clearer, more predictable terminology

Redesigning the Booking Experience

New Booking is HQ’s primary workflow, but the existing experience separates closely related decisions across multiple steps and exposes users to booking rules that are not always easy to understand.

Passenger InfoRide Details

Problems

  • The step’s name “Passenger Info” doesn’t reflect the content or the Package scenario
  • Passenger / Booker / Authorizing Employee relationships are difficult to understand
  • Secondary content—Admin Notes and Additional Emails—creates unnecessary complexity
  • Large map occupies valuable space before it becomes useful

Key UX Decisions

  • The step was renamed “Ride Details” to reflect its content
  • Clear separation between Passenger / Booker / Authorizing Employee
  • Secondary content—Admin Notes and Additional Emails—is placed inside a collapsible card
  • The map was removed from this step

Date and Location

Problems

  • The user can select Point to Point (Non-airport) and subsequently select an airport, creating contradictory booking information
  • Airport and potentially Meet & Greet functionality can appear in contexts that conflict with previous selections
  • Additional stops belong to the route, not Pickup

Key UX Decisions

  • The content shown reflects the selected Journey purpose
  • Meet & Greet is shown only when Airport is selected as the Journey purpose
  • Additional stops are placed logically between Pickup and Drop-off

Car and PaymentCar and Service

Problems

  • Unclear whose payment methods are being used
  • Booking-level actions can unexpectedly change account-level defaults (e.g. a newly added credit card becomes the default payment method)
  • The “Special Offer” tag doesn’t provide the type of information it represents
  • Important information that can affect the choice of service is hidden, with no visual cue when the section is collapsed

Key UX Decisions

  • Payment method moved to the Ride Details step, next to the authorizing employee it belongs to
  • New credit cards can now be added only in Account Settings
  • Offers and additional conditions are shown as badges with clear labels

Summary

Problems

  • Important conditions are missing. Cancellation policy, possible extra charges and the implications of a metered price should be visible before confirmation

Key UX Decisions

  • Added a short Preview and a full Details view
  • Important conditions are shown in both the Preview and Details views

Outcome

I reorganized HQ into five main areas centered on user tasks and redesigned the booking experience. Related tools now sit together, while Ride Details separates passenger, booker, and authorizing employee information and keeps secondary content collapsed.

What I learned
  • Understanding the relationships between people, permissions, and tasks was essential to simplifying HQ
  • The product audit helped me identify where clearer grouping, terminology, and information hierarchy could address complexity
What I would validate next
  • I would test whether users can find receipts, manage users, and complete bookings for themselves and others
  • I would track task completion, navigation errors, and confusion around booking roles to assess whether the proposed changes improve usability

These outcomes describe design changes; their impact on usability remains to be validated.
